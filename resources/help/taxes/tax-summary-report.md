# Using the tax summary when you file

The Tax summary report shows how much tax you collected, split by tax rate and by period. It is the number you or your accountant will use when preparing a return. Ledgerly does not file taxes for you.

## What does the report show?

For the date range you pick, the report lists one row per tax rate:

- **Rate name**, such as "State tax 6%".
- **Taxable sales**: the net amount the rate was applied to.
- **Tax collected**: the tax amount charged at that rate.
- **Invoices**: how many invoices used the rate.

Zero-rated lines appear with 0.00 tax, which helps if your return asks for zero-rated sales as a separate figure. Sales to tax-exempt clients are listed in a separate **Tax exempt** row.

## Which invoices are counted?

The report is based on the invoice date, not the payment date, and includes invoices with status Sent, Overdue and Paid. Draft invoices are ignored. Void invoices are excluded, because they were cancelled.

If your tax authority wants tax counted when payment is received, use the Payments received report alongside this one and ask your accountant how to reconcile them. Ledgerly shows both views but does not decide which method you should use.

## How do I prepare my figures for a return?

1. Open **Reports → Tax summary**.
2. Choose a date range that matches your filing period, for example **Quarter**. See Date ranges and time zones.
3. Check that every rate you expect appears in the list.
4. Compare the totals with your own records.
5. Export with **Export CSV** (Pro and Business) and send the file to your accountant.

If a rate looks wrong, open the invoices that used it from the report row and check the line items.

## What about credits and voided invoices?

A voided invoice does not count. Refunds issued after payment do not change the original invoice, so if you refund a taxed sale, your accountant may need to adjust the figure separately.

## Why does my total not match my records?

Common reasons are:

- The date range does not match your filing period.
- An invoice was dated in a different period from when you expected, which depends on your account time zone.
- A tax rate was renamed, so older invoices show a different name. See Changing a tax rate mid-year.
- Rounding differences, explained in Tax rounding rules.

For the report layout and options, see the Reports article called Tax summary report.
