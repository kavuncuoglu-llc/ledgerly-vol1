# Set default payment terms

Payment terms decide how many days a client has to pay. Ledgerly uses Net 30 by default, and you can change the account-wide default or set different terms for individual clients.

## What payment terms are available?

| Option | Due date |
| --- | --- |
| Due on receipt | The issue date |
| Net 7 | 7 days after the issue date |
| Net 14 | 14 days after the issue date |
| Net 15 | 15 days after the issue date |
| Net 30 | 30 days after the issue date (default) |
| Net 45 | 45 days after the issue date |
| Net 60 | 60 days after the issue date |
| Custom date | A specific date you choose |

The due date is calculated from the issue date, not the date you send the invoice. If you create a draft on the 1st and send it on the 5th with Net 30, the due date is still 30 days after the 1st unless you change the issue date.

## How do I change the account default?

1. Open **Settings → Invoices**.
2. Find **Default payment terms**.
3. Pick an option from the menu.
4. Select **Save changes**.

The new default applies to invoices you create from now on. Existing invoices keep their due dates. On the same page you can edit the invoice number prefix and the next number, and set the **Minimum invoice amount**.

## How do I set terms for one client?

Open **Clients**, select the client, then **Edit**. Under **Default payment terms**, choose a value, or leave it on **Use account default**. A client's setting wins over the account default whenever you create an invoice for them. This is useful when one client always pays on receipt and another needs Net 60.

## Can I change the terms on a single invoice?

Yes. In the invoice form, change the **Payment terms** menu or pick **Custom date** and choose any calendar date. Changing terms on one invoice does not alter the client's or account's default. After an invoice is sent, editing the due date requires you to resend it so your client sees the new date.

## How do payment terms affect reminders and late fees?

Reminders and late fees are measured from the due date. The Overdue status is set automatically the day after the due date, at around 1 a.m. in your account's time zone. On Pro and Business, automatic reminders go out at 3, 7 and 14 days past due, and a late fee is applied 30 days after the due date by default.

Shorter terms bring money in sooner, so many freelancers choose Net 14 or Net 15 once they have a few repeat clients.
