# Collections best practices

Getting paid on time is mostly about clear terms, prompt follow-up, and making payment easy. This article suggests a practical routine using Ledgerly's built-in tools.

## How do I prevent late payments in the first place?

Most late payments come from unclear expectations or friction. Before an invoice is overdue:

- Set payment terms that fit the work. Net 14 suits many small jobs; see Choosing payment terms.
- Send the invoice as soon as the work is done, with the PDF and the secure payment link.
- Turn on both card and bank transfer (ACH) under **Settings → Payments** so clients can pay the way they prefer.
- Add every relevant billing contact (up to 3) so the invoice reaches whoever approves payment.
- Mention late fees in your invoice footer so there are no surprises.

## What follow-up routine works well?

Combine the automatic schedule with a few personal touches:

1. Day 0: invoice sent.
2. Optional courtesy reminder 3 days before the due date.
3. Automatic reminders at 3, 7 and 14 days past due.
4. At day 14 or 15, send a short personal email or call, since a person often succeeds where a reminder did not.
5. Around day 30 past due, a late fee applies automatically if you have fees turned on.

You can send one manual reminder per invoice every 24 hours. See Payment reminders for the full schedule.

## How should I use the Aging report?

Open **Reports → Aging** weekly. It groups unpaid invoices into current, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90 and 90+ days. Start with the oldest bucket, because the chance of collecting falls as invoices age. A client with several overdue invoices is a good candidate for a statement of account; see Sending a statement of account.

## When should I offer flexibility?

Pushing harder is not always the answer. If a client is cooperative but short of cash:

- Offer a payment plan of 2 to 12 installments. Late fees and reminders pause while it is on track. See Setting up a payment plan.
- Accept a partial payment now and record it with **Record payment**.
- Waive a late fee as a goodwill gesture with **⋯ → Waive late fee**. Waiving is permanent for that invoice.

If the client disputes the invoice, mark it as disputed so you do not send reminders in the middle of a conversation. See Disputing a charge.

## What if a client stops responding?

Keep your messages polite and factual. After repeated attempts:

- Send a final notice stating the amount, the invoice number and a clear deadline.
- Pause new work for that client until the balance is settled.
- Decide whether the amount justifies a formal collections process, which is outside Ledgerly.

Ledgerly does not give legal advice. If an invoice will never be paid, you can void it, which keeps the record but cancels the amount owed. See Voiding an invoice.
