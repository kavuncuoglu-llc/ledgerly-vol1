# Disputing a charge

If a client questions an invoice, you can mark it as Disputed so Ledgerly stops reminders and late fees while you sort it out. This article also explains how card chargebacks differ from invoice disputes.

## How do I mark an invoice as disputed?

1. Open **Invoices** and select the invoice.
2. Choose **⋯ → Mark as disputed**.
3. Confirm.

The invoice keeps its normal status (Sent or Overdue), and a Disputed flag appears next to it. While the flag is on, automatic reminders and late fees are paused. Only you can see the flag; clients do not.

## How do I clear the disputed flag?

Open the invoice and choose **⋯ → Clear dispute**. Reminders resume with the next one that falls due. Late fees resume from the day the flag is removed. Ledgerly does not back-charge the time the invoice was paused, so a dispute that lasted three weeks adds no fees for those three weeks.

If the dispute ends with a smaller amount owed, edit the invoice if it is still a Draft, or void it and issue a corrected invoice. See Voiding an invoice.

## Late fee disputes

A client may dispute the late fee itself rather than the original amount. You have two options:

- **Waive the fee.** Open the invoice and choose **⋯ → Waive late fee**. Waiving is permanent for that invoice, so no further fees will be charged on it.
- **Pause the fee.** Mark the invoice as disputed while you review the case. No new fees accrue until you clear the flag, and the paused days are never charged.

If you only want to lower the rate for future invoices, see Changing the late-fee percentage. If the client needs more time instead, a payment plan may be a better fit; see Setting up a payment plan.

## What is the difference between a dispute and a chargeback?

An invoice dispute is a flag inside Ledgerly that you control. A chargeback is different: the client asks their card issuer to reverse a card payment, and the issuer decides the outcome.

- Chargebacks are handled from **Dashboard → Payments → Disputes**.
- There is a $15 dispute fee for each chargeback.
- Respond with your evidence, such as the invoice, the receipt, and any emails with the client, within the deadline shown on the dispute.

Because chargebacks cost time and money, it helps to answer client questions about an invoice quickly and to issue a refund when one is clearly warranted. Refunds can be full or partial within 90 days; see Refunding a payment.

## Can I dispute a charge from Ledgerly itself?

If you have a question about an invoice from Ledgerly for your own subscription, email support@ledgerly.example with the invoice number. See Billing for your Ledgerly subscription for how those invoices work.
