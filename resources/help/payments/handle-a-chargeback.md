# Handle a chargeback

A chargeback happens when a client asks their bank to reverse a card payment instead of contacting you. You manage them in **Dashboard → Payments → Disputes**, and each one carries a $15 dispute fee.

## What is a chargeback?

A chargeback, also called a dispute, is filed by the cardholder with their bank. The bank takes the disputed amount back from your balance while the case is reviewed. Typical reasons are an unrecognised charge, a claim that services were not delivered, or a duplicate payment.

Chargebacks apply to card payments. They are separate from marking an invoice as "Disputed" in Ledgerly, which only pauses reminders and late fees on your side and has nothing to do with the bank.

## How will I find out?

When a dispute opens, Ledgerly emails the account owner and shows a banner on the Dashboard. The dispute appears under **Dashboard → Payments → Disputes** with the invoice number, client, amount, the reason given by the bank and the deadline to respond.

## How do I respond?

1. Open **Dashboard → Payments → Disputes** and click the dispute.
2. Read the reason code and the deadline.
3. Click **Submit evidence**.
4. Upload supporting files, such as the signed agreement, delivery proof, emails with the client, or the invoice PDF. Attach up to 5 files, 10 MB each.
5. Write a short factual summary of the work and the agreement.
6. Click **Send to bank**.

Respond before the deadline shown. If you miss it, the dispute is lost automatically. Evidence can be submitted once, so gather everything first.

## What does a chargeback cost?

Ledgerly charges a $15 dispute fee per chargeback. In addition, the disputed amount is withdrawn from your balance while the bank decides. The original processing fee on that payment is not returned. See Processing fees for online payments.

## What happens to the invoice?

While the dispute is open the invoice keeps its status. If you lose the dispute, the payment is reversed and the invoice returns to Sent or Overdue with the full balance outstanding again. If you win, the money is restored and nothing changes.

To avoid reminders reaching a client in the middle of a dispute, open the invoice and use **⋯ → Mark as disputed**. Clear the flag once it is resolved. Late fees resume from the day you remove the flag, without back-charging.

## How can I prevent chargebacks?

- Use a clear business name so your charges are recognised.
- Send invoices promptly with a description of the work.
- Reply quickly when a client questions a charge.
- Offer a refund early if a client is unhappy. See Issue a refund.
